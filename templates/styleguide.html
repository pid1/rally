Calendar month grid

Today is marked with a rule, never a fill: the palette is greyscale, so the only colour on the page is the family-member dots and a filled cell would shout over them. A day that overflows offers “+N more” and switches to that day’s agenda — a popover would be a second modal chassis. The grid renders at every width. It was once hidden below 768px on a measurement of 42px per column, but that was taken at 320px; re-measured, a 390px phone gives 51px and the grid earns its place. On a phone the cell grows to fit its events and the rows carry text rather than dots, each row a target in its own right — an event row that cannot be 44px must not be a target at all, and pointer-events: none is not that: it stops the finger and leaves the keyboard.

Calendar time grid

Day and Week are one component with the column count set to 1 or 7. It exists for what a list cannot say: duration is height and adjacency is position, so a collision is visible rather than arithmetic. Three rules hold it together.

The hour is 5.5rem so that thirty minutes is exactly 44px. The shortest a body may be and the hit-area floor are pinned to each other; that is the whole reason for the value. Starts and heights snap to five minutes.

An event under thirty minutes is two shapes, not one. A tab down the left carries the event’s real length and the member colour; the body beside it holds the words at the thirty-minute minimum. Inflating the whole block instead made a quarter-hour claim three quarters of one. Tab plus body is exactly the column width, and both sit inside one button, so it stays a single target.

Columns are packed on the painted rectangle, not the true span — otherwise a floored body sits on top of the next event and covers its own title. All-day events leave the time axis entirely and span the band above it. Today is a rule and the now line is a hairline, for the same reason the month grid marks today that way.
